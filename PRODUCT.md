# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Comic book fans across the spectrum: casual walk-ins who want to check deals and events, regulars managing active pull lists, and serious collectors tracking pre-orders, CGC graded books, and wish lists. The admin user (store owner/staff) manages inventory and rewards from the same app. The app must feel natural to all three customer types without making any of them feel like an afterthought.

## Product Purpose

A mobile companion for Duncanville Comics, Toys and Collectibles — a local brick-and-mortar comic shop. Customers browse weekly releases, manage personal pull lists and wish lists, earn and redeem store rewards, and stay up to date on events. The store uses it to push weekly inventory, run the rewards program, and stay connected to their regulars. Success means regulars check it every Wednesday.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, accessible, fun. The energy of a shop run by people who love what they sell — not a chain store, not a corporate app. It should feel like the people behind the counter: knowledgeable, enthusiastic, and genuinely glad you're here.

## Anti-references

- **Amazon / eBay**: Cold, transactional product grids with no personality. Zero warmth.
- **Target / Walmart retail apps**: Over-templated, sterile, indistinguishable from any other retail app.
- **Overcrowded fan wikis / community sites**: Too much information density, chaotic layouts, dark backgrounds on everything. Exhausting to navigate.

## Design Principles

1. **Local, not retail**: Every screen should feel like it came from the actual store, not a white-label app template. The store's identity — name, voice, red-and-white palette — should be present and intentional, not just a skin on a generic shell.
2. **Clarity at every level**: Casual browsers should be able to orient instantly; pull list regulars should be able to act fast. Reveal complexity progressively — don't front-load the full feature set.
3. **Fun earns its place**: Personality comes through in copy, empty states, and moments of delight — not decorative clutter. Every visual "fun" element should justify its presence.
4. **The regulars come first**: Wednesday is the store's big day. The app should make it effortless to check new releases, confirm what's ready in your pull list, and take action. Optimize for that flow above all others.
5. **Consistent confidence**: Same patterns, same words, same weights throughout. A component that looks like a primary action on one screen shouldn't look tertiary on the next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Readable at all ages — the comic shop audience skews wide. Touch targets 44x44px minimum. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No meaning conveyed by color alone.
